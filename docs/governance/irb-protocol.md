## 1. Background and rationale

Short behavioural games can capture how people make everyday choices (holding back an action, adapting to a
change of rules, persisting with a hard problem, sharing with others) without reading, writing or
questionnaires. This matters in India, where many adults have limited literacy and standard questionnaires
work poorly. However, game-based measures need evidence that they are reliable, that they relate to
established measures, and that they work fairly across languages, literacy levels, ages and genders. The
literature suggests modest correlations between behavioural tasks and self-reports (about .05 to .20) and
moderate test-retest reliability (about .3 to .6), so the study expects only some of the 17 constructs to
reach the standard needed for a participant summary, possibly none in the first wave. The protocol is
designed to find out which, and to say so openly.

## 2. Aims

1. Establish that the games are understandable without reading, in each pilot language (Stage 1).
2. Estimate the reliability and validity of game indicators against a standard psychometric criterion
   battery, and compare two game formats (Stage 2).
3. Estimate test-retest stability over 14 to 28 days (Stage 3).
4. Test fairness: measurement invariance and differential item functioning across language, literacy,
   education, age, gender, device familiarity, input mode and site mode (Stage 4).
5. Estimate incremental validity for the outcomes defined by the report-use decision, and build provisional
   norms (Stage 5).

Hypotheses, thresholds and multiplicity corrections are pre-registered before Stage 2 (generated by
`tools/prereg` from `scoring/hypotheses.yaml` and `scoring/gates.yaml`; the registration id is added here when
assigned).

### 3.1 Stage details

- **Stage 1 / 1b (think-aloud).** A researcher unlocks think-aloud mode (pauses allowed, no time-outs).
  The participant plays while saying what they think; an observer codes confusion, misunderstood
  instructions, reading-dependent screens, digit-dependent decisions, cultural mismatch and strategy.
  Comprehension of the consent and of the picture summary is tested with **demo profiles only**. Exit
  criteria include zero reading-dependent screens and at least 80% summary comprehension.
- **Stage 2 (pilot).** Game session (up to 15 minutes) plus the criterion battery (questionnaires, a Tower of
  London board, a card-sort task, an administrator-rated cooperation task). Calibration level fixed for
  comparability. Only provisional indicator flags at exit.
- **Stage 2-D (co-play).** An optional fourth episode with a real partner, sessions up to 20 minutes, separate
  consent per player, private partner ratings.
- **Stage 3 (retest).** Participants who agreed to be contacted are invited by a pre-recorded voice call (SMS
  optional), play a parallel form at the same level, and answer a short criterion block.
- **Stage 4 (fairness).** Analyses of Stage 2 to 3 data plus the unattended-mode substudy (S4u), which uses
  open public kiosks in thank-you mode with core consent only.
- **Stage 5.** Outcome capture (supervisor or peer rating, or an observed task, depending on the report-use
  decision), incremental validity, provisional norms.

## 4. Participants

**Inclusion:** adults aged 18 or over (self-declared); able to hear the audio in one of the enabled
languages; able to use a touch screen with or without the accessibility profile; for S2 to S5, attending a
supervised site and willing to give criterion consent.
**Exclusion:** under 18 (the session ends at the age question and nothing is kept); unable to give consent;
previous participation in the same study stage (the "played before" question and pid checks).

**Vulnerable participants.** Many participants have limited literacy; consent is given by audio in their own
language with pictograms and a comprehension check (supervised sites). Older adults are included; the
accessibility profile and practice ensure the tasks are usable. No children. No participant is recruited
through an employer or any authority that could pressure them. **[IEC REVIEW]**

## 5. Sampling, quotas and recruitment

- **Quota cells:** language x age band x gender x education band x literacy band. A "cell full" warning
  stops over-recruitment; arms are block-randomised within cells.
- **Languages:** three pilot languages from two language families and two scripts (placeholders hi-IN,
  mr-IN, ta-IN). **[STAKEHOLDER REVIEW]**
- **Recruitment:** at community sites (for example markets, community centres, training centres) by trained
  field staff, using a spoken invitation script; no cold calling. Open public kiosks (S4u) are self-selected.
- **Compensation:** [TBD: amount and form] for time and travel at supervised sites, paid whether or not the
  participant completes the session, and not tied to optional consents. **[IEC REVIEW]**

## 6. Consent process

Scripts: [consent/](consent/README.md). Summary:

- Game play: pre-recorded audio consent in four segments with pictograms; only a tap commits; archive opt-in
  is separate (default no); contact opt-in after the summary (default no).
- Criterion battery, co-play substudy, ASR recordings and think-aloud audio each have their own consent,
  read by trained staff, with a comprehension check.
- Consent records store the exact audio version played (SHA-256).
- No written signature is collected; the IEC is asked to approve audio consent with a tap as the
  affirmative action, documented electronically, under ICMR provisions for minimal-risk research with
  participants who may not read. **[IEC REVIEW] [LEGAL REVIEW]**

## 8. Risks and how they are reduced

The study is assessed as **minimal risk**.

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Fatigue or boredom | Short session, time budget enforced, breaks allowed, stop at any time |
| Mild frustration (a rude customer in the stall scenario, an unsolvable puzzle, an unfair loss) | Scenarios are mild and fictional; tested in Stage 1 for acceptability; debriefing explains unsolvable puzzles; participants can stop |
| Discomfort with honesty-related scenarios (bribe offer, private dice report) | Fictional market scenarios; the true dice roll is never recorded; integrity is analysed for groups only and never shown to a participant |
| Privacy (onlookers at kiosks) | No scores on screen, no numbers in the summary, phone digits never spoken on speakers |
| Loss of confidentiality | Pseudonymous ids, separate identity vault, encryption, access control, retention limits ([DPIA](dpia.md)) |
| Misinterpreting the summary | No summary until reliability and fairness gates pass; strengths-framed bands; "a snapshot, not a diagnosis"; personal reflection only ([report-use statement](report-use-statement.md)) |
| Questionnaire items touching on emotions (for example difficulties with emotion regulation) | Standard, non-clinical instruments; items can be skipped; administrators trained to respond to distress and to give the helpline information [TBD: referral resources per site] **[IEC REVIEW]** |

## 9. Deception and debriefing

- **Scripted partners.** In the episodes, some characters are computer-controlled partners. They are
  introduced as "pretend helpers" (`partner_disclosure`), so participants are not misled about who they play
  with.
- **Unsolvable puzzle and planned difficulty.** One puzzle (persistence measure) cannot be solved, and some
  difficulty changes are unannounced (adaptability measure). This is incomplete disclosure, justified because
  announcing it would invalidate the measure, and the risk is minimal.
- **Debriefing.** At supervised sites, every participant hears a short debriefing at the end (script in
  [coplay_substudy.md](consent/coplay_substudy.md), section "Deception and debriefing"), including that the
  unsolvable puzzle says nothing bad about them, and is offered deletion of their data. At open public kiosks
  (S4u), a short spoken debriefing line plays at the end of the session.
- **[IEC REVIEW]** approve the level of disclosure for scripted partners and the debriefing wording.

## 10. Benefits

No direct benefit is promised. When the release gates pass, participants may receive a short picture summary
for personal reflection. The broader benefit is assessment tools that work for adults with limited literacy
in Indian languages.

## 11. Confidentiality and data management

See the [data management plan](data-management-plan.md), [DPIA](dpia.md),
[retention and deletion policy](retention-and-deletion-policy.md) and
[access control policy](access-control-policy.md). Key points: pseudonymous ids; no names in research data;
contact details only in a separate vault and only on opt-in; no audio kept from play; research audio only
with separate consent and deleted after coding; India-region hosting; erasure on request within 72 hours.

## 12. Data sharing and publication

Aggregate results will be published, including null and negative results, with confirmatory and exploratory
analyses clearly separated. De-identified data from participants who gave archive consent may be shared with
approved researchers under a data-use agreement (k-anonymity >= 5). No individual-level results are published.

## 13. Adverse events, incidents and complaints

- Participant distress: the administrator stops the session, offers support and the helpline; the event is
  logged and the Ethics Officer is informed within 24 hours.
- Data incidents: [incident response runbook](incident-response-runbook.md); the IEC is informed as an
  unanticipated problem.
- Complaints: helpline and site desk; the Ethics Officer receives every complaint within 24 hours; the
  complaint log is part of the evidence pack.

## 14. Monitoring, amendments and reporting

- The Ethics Officer reviews consent records, complaints, erasure statistics and incidents monthly.
- Any change to procedures, consent wording, data collected, retention or report use is submitted to the IEC
  as an amendment before use.
- Progress reports to the IEC at the interval it sets, and a final report at study close.
